# Scanner competitors and subscription economics, 2026-09-28

**Purpose:** bounded primary-source evidence for [Derive monetization](../MONETIZATION.md). U.S. publicly visible web and App Store claims were checked on 2026-09-28. App Store lists multiple in-app purchase products, including legacy or promotional SKUs; a listing is not the price offered to a particular user. Vendor descriptions are claims, not independent capability tests. “Unknown” means the cited public source did not establish it. No paid signup, app trial, private account, or customer data was used.

The closest counterexample to “more scans sells Plus” is HadaBuddy's unlimited free scanning and routines; Yuka and INCI Beauty also keep core scanning useful without an expensive subscription. Think Dirty and OnSkin show that some competitors do meter Free scans. SkinSort's whole-routine insights support cross-product analysis as a paid category, but its comparison help also documents a useful free Compare path. Derive should test its own incremental value and cost instead of importing another app's quota or score model. These are inferences from the linked vendor pages, not evidence of their conversion or Derive's likely conversion.

**Visible customer sentiment, anecdotal:** Think Dirty's own FAQ reproduces a user complaint that previously free information moved behind a paywall; one complaint does not establish prevalence, but it highlights the trust cost of changing access without clear notice. A [SkinSort App Store review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/skinsort-the-skincare-app/id6478040418) praises the usefulness of free comparison, ingredient detail, and routine tools while describing Premium as optional. An [OnSkin App Store review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/onskin-beauty-product-scanner/id1630768985) praises broad scanning and a paid routine. These selected public reviews are customer anecdotes, not representative survey data or proof of paid retention. [Think Dirty FAQ](https://www.thinkdirtyapp.com/faqs/).

## Subscription-app benchmarks and limits of transfer

[RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps) reports from its subscription-platform sample, not from Derive's audience. Its D35 download-to-paid medians are **2.1% freemium** and **10.7% hard paywall**, with similar reported first-year retention by access method. Health & Fitness D35 median is **2.9%** across access methods and the U.S. App Store category labels do not guarantee Derive belongs to the same demand pattern. A hard paywall selects for immediate purchase and gives fewer people scanner first value; these observational medians do not show that making Derive paid at launch would produce five times its paid users.

RevenueCat reports median Health & Fitness download-to-trial **6.9%** and trial-to-paid **37.7%**. Across categories, trials of at most four days had **25.5%** median trial-to-paid versus **42.5%** for 17–32 days; those cohorts differ in product and offer design. Its 2026 price medians are **$8 monthly** and **$34.80 annually** overall, **$9.99 monthly** for Health & Fitness, and **$9.99 monthly / $39.99 annually** in North America across stores. The first-year retained-subscriber medians for the reported cohort were **8% monthly** and **28% annual**. These definitions matter: trial-to-paid is among trial starters, download-to-paid is among installs, and first-year retention is among paid subscriptions. None is a credible Derive forecast before a real cohort exists. [RevenueCat methodology and tables](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps).

**Price challenge:** `$4.99/month` is below RevenueCat's Health & Fitness monthly median but close to HadaBuddy. `$30–40/year` sits near the overall annual median yet gives a 33–50% discount against twelve months at $4.99. Competitor sticker prices are poor substitutes for Derive willingness to pay; proposed Plus features and low-cost acquisition must justify the price. **Trial challenge:** a 60-day Founding Plus grant can demonstrate use but cannot yield rapid paid conversion evidence within a 24–48 hour release push. If Plus is not present, a “Plus trial” has no measurable treatment.

## Referrals, creators, and offers

[Apple campaign links](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/acquisition/campaign-links) allow privacy-conscious campaign-level views of App Store impressions, downloads, usage, sales, and subscriptions. This can separate club/creator/source campaigns but does not itself prove person-level referral credit or incrementality. [RevenueCat's referral guide](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/how-to-build-a-referral-program-for-mobile-apps) says to start small and warns that referrals are no growth shortcut. [Apple subscription offer codes](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/set-up-subscription-offer-codes) can grant a free or discounted period and may then renew at standard price depending on setup, making expiration and renewal copy a product requirement. These are possible later mechanisms, not approved campaigns or current Derive implementation.

For a bounded first experiment, give each club/creator a distinct campaign source, log aggregate first value and repeat useful Checks, then compare eligible paid and Managed-interest outcomes with the broader cohort. Record creator compensation separately from attribution; no claim of paid growth, commission return, or viral coefficient is supported yet. Preserve privacy and independence in share links and product recommendations.

## Evidence still missing for a commercial decision

1. Derive cohort-level first useful Check, repeat use, and cross-product demand, segmented by acquisition source and denied/unknown product outcomes.
2. Actual cost per completed customer Check by input type, plus Plus-active-user support and provider costs.
3. Live paywall offer, purchase, refund, renewal, and cancellation observations; App Store SKU lists alone cannot supply these.
4. Founder-reviewed Plus benefit, Managed service capacity, and qualitative refusal reasons.
5. Verified merchant listings, offer freshness, permitted attribution, and payout terms before any Shop revenue projection.
